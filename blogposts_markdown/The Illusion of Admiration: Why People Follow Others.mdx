In the age of social media, where millions follow influencers, entrepreneurs, and thought leaders, a fundamental truth remains hidden beneath layers of admiration and appreciation — people like themselves the most. They follow others not out of pure admiration but because they see a reflection of their own desires, ambitions, and aspirations.

## The Self-Interest Principle

At its core, human behavior is driven by self-interest. We connect with people who inspire us, not because we truly admire them as individuals, but because we see them as stepping stones to becoming who we want to be. This is what I call the Self-Interest Principle — the idea that people are drawn to others who align with their own aspirations, benefiting from their knowledge, influence, or success.

Think about it: when someone follows a fitness influencer, is it because they care about that person’s life? Not really. They follow them because they want to be fit themselves. When someone follows a billionaire entrepreneur, is it out of love for their personality? No. It is because they want to unlock the secrets of success and wealth.

This principle extends beyond social media. In friendships, relationships, and workplaces, people form bonds where mutual benefits exist. Even when someone claims to “love” or “admire” another person, there is an underlying self-interest — whether it is companionship, emotional support, inspiration, or knowledge.

## Why Do People Follow Influencers?

The massive followings of influencers, celebrities, and leaders can be explained by this same idea. People do not follow influencers because they deeply care about their lives; they follow because they see value in their content, whether it’s entertainment, motivation, or education. They seek a roadmap, a direction, or even a shortcut to their own goals.

In essence, admiration is a disguised form of self-interest. Even in the grandest gestures of support, there is a silent, subconscious voice asking: How does this benefit me?

## “Riches Are Built on the Shoulders of Giants”

There is an old saying: “Riches are built on the shoulders of giants.” Every successful individual, every entrepreneur, every artist, and every leader has, in some way, learned from those who came before them. They have studied their journeys, absorbed their wisdom, and used their influence to carve their own paths.

Social media has amplified this principle. People follow the “giants” of their field not to admire them blindly but to extract the knowledge and strategies that can lead them to similar success. The cycle continues — those who were once followers become leaders, and new followers seek guidance from them.

## The Reality Behind Likes and Followers

Does this mean people are selfish? Not necessarily. It simply means that human nature is deeply rooted in self-improvement. The next time you gain a follower or someone expresses admiration, ask yourself — what is the value they are seeking? They may appreciate your work, but ultimately, they see something in you that aligns with their own journey.

And perhaps, that is not a flaw but the very essence of progress.
