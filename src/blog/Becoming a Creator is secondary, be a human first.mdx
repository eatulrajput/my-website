## 1. Creator is Not Just a Word
“Creator” is not like a word such as potato, which is used everywhere. It is often thrown around casually, but at its core, being a creator is about skill, mastery, and intrinsic motivation.

Psychology research shows that intrinsic motivation, doing something because it is inherently satisfying rather than for external rewards is a strong predictor of creativity and long-term engagement. If you are a creator, it means you are someone who is motivated to develop and express your specialized skills and share them with others.

## 2. Sharing Your Work
Creation is one part; sharing is another. The internet provides platforms to showcase your work:

Videos: platforms like YouTube or Instagram, depending on the content duration.
Visual graphics: LinkedIn, Dribbble, Twitter, Instagram, depending on your audience and type of content.
The platform affects reach, engagement, and growth, but your motivation should remain internal rather than platform-driven.

## 3. The Algorithm Factor
Algorithms on social media are designed to maximize user engagement and ad revenue, not necessarily to support creators. Research in neuroscience shows that dopamine, a neurotransmitter linked to reward, spikes when we receive likes, comments, or new followers. This creates a feedback loop where creators may experience temporary pleasure, but also anxiety or frustration when engagement drops.

Understanding this helps maintain intrinsic motivation rather than becoming dependent on external validation.

## 4. Society and Social Pressure
Humans are social beings, and social comparison is natural. Psychologists have found that comparing oneself to peers can lead to both motivation and negative emotions like envy.

For example, friends may outwardly say, “Go ahead, be a creator,” but inwardly feel envy or doubt. Society often pressures individuals to stick to traditional career paths, making breaking free from the 9-to-5 loop socially challenging. Awareness of these dynamics can help you navigate them without losing focus.

## 5. Doing Extra Work
Being a creator requires managing multiple responsibilities. Cognitive science shows that multitasking and divided attention can increase cognitive load and stress. You are effectively doing extra work:

Your job to sustain income.
Creating additional content (videos, graphics, blogs).
Sharing your work online and engaging with the audience.
This requires discipline, time management, and emotional resilience. You also need social capital, support from friends, family, and your network to sustain this work without burnout.

## 6. Inner Clarity Comes First
To manage work, creation, social life, and personal well-being, you need self-awareness and clarity. Research in psychology shows that people who reflect on their values, goals, and identity experience greater life satisfaction and decision-making quality.

Ask yourself:

Who am I?
What do I want in life?
How do I want to live my life?
Without this, people risk regret and burnout, as unexamined decisions often lead to dissatisfaction later in life. Developing self-awareness first allows you to navigate challenges consciously and intentionally.

## 7. Be Human First
Be human first, then be the doer. Conscious action matters more than mechanical action. Just doing things without reflection makes you part of the algorithmic loop, where your work is shaped more by external forces than by your own purpose.
