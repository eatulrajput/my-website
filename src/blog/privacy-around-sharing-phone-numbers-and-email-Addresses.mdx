Privacy Around Sharing Phone Numbers and Email Addresses
</BlogTitle>

<BlogContent>
Why should we share our phone number or email address so freely, when many people are actively trying to spam us, push advertisements, or even exploit systems for their own gain?

At the end of the day, how much privacy you want to maintain is your decision. But in my opinion, it’s always better to be cautious than to get attacked first and then look for a cure. As the saying goes, prevention is better than cure.

<BlogImage src="/images/blog/privacy/privacy-1.png" />

## Things worth thinking about:
- Be careful with sharing your phone number.
Don’t share your phone number online without a clear reason. And if you do share it, make sure you trust the person or service enough to believe it won’t be passed around carelessly.

- A phone number today is not just a way to call someone. That idea is almost two decades old. Now, your phone number is linked to banking, social media, government services, and almost every digital platform you use. Once it’s leaked or misused, the impact can be much larger than people expect.

- I often see people adding their phone numbers to their resumes, and it makes me think. If a recruiter is genuinely busy and serious about hiring, they are far more likely to reach out via email first. Calling someone directly is rarely the initial step. From that perspective, sharing a phone number on a resume doesn’t always make practical sense to me.

Deal with spam actively.
Block spammers whenever possible and avoid giving your number to services that don’t really need it. When you have a choice, using email instead of a phone number is usually safer.


<BlogImage src="/images/blog/privacy/privacy-2.png" />

## About email
Email is one of the best tools humans have built for communication, and it’s everywhere — job applications, social media, subscriptions, and professional networking. But just because it’s common doesn’t mean it should be used carelessly.

Email providers today are much more secure than they were in the past. They use advanced filtering and machine learning to protect users from threats. Still, that doesn’t mean we can ignore basic cybersecurity awareness. Knowing how to recognize suspicious links, fake messages, or unusual requests is becoming a necessary skill, and it will only matter more in the future.

I’ll wrap up here.
The internet offers incredible opportunities, but it also comes with risks. The choices you make about privacy today can save you from many problems tomorrow.
